Learning Contact-Rich Humanoid Loco-Manipulation without Paired Interaction Demonstrations

Abstract

Contact-rich humanoid loco-manipulation requires adapting whole-body coordination and contact forces to object conditions. Existing methods relying on paired humanoid–object interaction (HOI) demonstrations are limited by the difficulty of collecting diverse interaction data at scale and may inherit restrictions from the demonstrated contact patterns. To address these limitations, we propose LORE, a framework for learning such loco-manipulation skills without paired robot–object trajectories or reference-derived contact sequences. LORE jointly learns motion skills and a compact latent representation through variational reinforcement learning, with an arm-load curriculum that prepares the abilities for sustained interaction loads. Downstream task policies adapt these skills through the pretrained prior–decoder interface, using object-centric kinematic and contact-force rewards to learn contact-rich loco-manipulation behaviors. Extensive simulation and hardware experiments demonstrate contact-rich humanoid loco-manipulation across diverse object properties and spatial conditions, while showing adaptation of whole-body contact patterns to these variations.
